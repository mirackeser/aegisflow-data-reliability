# Profiling evaluation

Generated by `python -m aegisflow_dr.profiling_eval`; a test fails if this file drifts from the code.

**Scope.** Deterministic profiling checks against synthetic injected faults in generated series. Not real sensors, not unseen fault kinds, and not a language model.

Corpus: 60 base series x 11 variants, 300 records each. Outlier threshold K = 4.5 (second differences, median-absolute-deviation scale).

Rates are `found/total = percent [95% Wilson interval]`; `n/a` means there was nothing to measure.

## How the threshold was chosen (development split only)

K was fixed before the held-out split was scored, from a design target of about 1e-5 false flags per clean Gaussian value (P(|z|>K), so K of 4.5 or more), and was not changed afterwards. Lower K finds more small spikes and eventually raises false alarms; higher K does the opposite. The measured false-alarm rate is reported below and is higher than that target.

**Measured false alarms on clean series (all splits):** 1/54000 = 0.0019% [0–0.01] (percent of scanned numeric values flagged). Expect roughly that many false outlier flags on noise-only data; real sensor noise is usually heavier-tailed, so expect more.

## Reading these numbers

- Small spikes (4 to 6 noise units) and small level shifts (3 to 5 noise units) are only partly found by design; the first and last value of a series are never flagged as outliers.
- A level shift is a persistent step (for example a recalibrated or replaced sensor). Before the `level_shift` check existed, 43 of 60 series with a large step were misread as an isolated jump, often on the wrong record; that is why these kinds were added. Held-out had already been scored for the other kinds when they were added, so the level-shift rows are the first and only look at held-out for those two kinds, and no threshold was changed for them (the step check reuses K and a partner ratio fixed in advance).
- `null_burst` is scored per column, not per record, because the finding lists at most 20 records.
- Every fault was injected by the same authors who wrote the checks, into one family of generated series (trend, one seasonal wave, Gaussian noise). High scores here mean the checks do what they were written to do on that family. They are not evidence about real sensors, heavier-tailed noise, other fault kinds, or any language model.
