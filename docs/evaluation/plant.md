# Simulated plant incident loop evaluation

Generated by `python -m aegisflow_dr.plant_eval` (development, validation and held-out (`--include-heldout`)).

**Scope.** Simulated faults injected into a simulated plant and recognised by detectors and probes written by the same authors. These rates measure internal consistency of the incident loop, not real-world diagnosis. No root cause, no model, no production connection.

## Method

- **Cases.** For every seed and scenario a fresh simulated plant runs a seeded warm-up of 60-599 live seconds, then one fault is injected. The plant advances one monitoring check (15 s) at a time until an incident opens on the fault's sensor, or on the stream for stream faults, within 900 s.
- **Evidence.** The plant keeps advancing until the incident's evidence is frozen, within 600 s. The evidence is the first full 200 s window after the change onset estimated from the readings (`changepoint`).
- **Probes.** `hypotheses.investigate`, the call the agents' A24 makes, runs on the frozen evidence.
- **Step-1 comparison.** The same probe loop also runs on the window in which the incident was first detected: what step 1 investigated when an investigation was started right at detection. Step 1 kept the latest window with findings, so a later start saw more post-change readings; this column is its earliest case, not its average.
- **Splits** are fixed by seed: development 1-20, on which the estimator and the evidence rule were designed; validation 21-40; held-out 41-60, scored once after review.
- **Changes after validation.** The first validation run showed two weaknesses, and two changes followed before held-out was scored. The onset of a stuck seasonal sensor was misplaced, because one common harmonic was removed before splitting, so each segment now has its own harmonic. Noise faults closed before their evidence was complete, so collection now continues after an incident closes. Validation is therefore not independent for these two changes; held-out is.
- **Thresholds.** Every threshold (change point: minimum segment 10, clear at a statistic of 30; seasonal R² 0.5; the probe thresholds of F04) was fixed before validation and held-out were scored. None was changed in response to these numbers.

Columns:

- *Detected* — an incident opened on the right sensor or stream.
- *Onset ±5s* — the estimated onset is within 5 s of the injection.
- *Evidence ready* — the evidence froze before the limit.
- *Sole supported* — the expected mechanism is the only supported candidate on the frozen evidence.
- *Wrong supported* — any other mechanism, except `genuine_change`, is supported.
- *Step 1* — the sole-supported rate on the first detection window. Its denominator is the detected cases, while *Sole supported* counts cases whose evidence froze.

Rates are `count/cases = percent [Wilson 95%]`, and `n/a` means nothing to measure.

## Reading these numbers

- Simulated faults only, with fixed magnitudes, and detectors and probes written by the same authors. High rates show that the loop recognises these faults, not how it would do on real sensors.
- **Noise on `temp_c`.** The F04 spread probe compares marginal spreads, and on a seasonal sensor the seasonal swing dominates them. A threefold noise increase raises the spread by only about 20%, so the probe refutes the noise mechanism. This is a limit of the fixed probe, not tuned away here.
- **Duplicate burst.** The burst repeats 30 readings, about 13% of a 200 s window, which is below the fixed 20% support threshold of `duplicate_share`, so the candidate stays open. Its onset is the start of the repeated range, 29 s before the injection, by construction.
- **Stuck sensor.** Its onset is one second before the injection: the stuck value is the previous reading.
- **Ingestion pause.** Its onset is the second after the last reading (`data_stopped`), not a change point.
- "Supported" means consistent with fixed probes and inconsistent with none. It never means the cause.
- Seeds are independent plants. Scenarios of one seed share the plant's noise stream up to the warm-up, so rates across scenarios are not fully independent.
